# Elora business model

## The business in one sentence

Elora helps event organizers sell tickets, event merchandise and partner inventory in one branded checkout, while giving attendees one trusted place to discover and manage the full event experience.

## Recommended model: B2B2C event commerce platform

Elora serves two audiences, but they do not have equal priority at the beginning:

- **Primary customer: event organizers.** They bring events to the platform and pay for the infrastructure and distribution.
- **End customer: attendees.** They discover events, buy tickets and merch, and manage their orders.
- **Expansion customers: vendors, sponsors and promoters.** They use the event audience and operational tools once the organizer workflow is proven.

This is stronger than presenting Elora as only a ticketing website. Ticketing is the entry product; event commerce and operations are the larger business.

## The problem Elora solves

Organizers often have to combine several disconnected tools:

- A ticket link or payment page
- Social media for discovery
- Spreadsheets for ticket inventory
- Separate merch orders
- Manual vendor and sponsor coordination
- Manual refund and customer support work
- Unclear payout and commission records

Attendees experience the same fragmentation from the other side: one link for a ticket, another for merchandise, and little visibility into their order after payment.

## Elora's selling point

> **One event, one checkout, one operating system.**

For attendees:

> Discover the event, secure your ticket and get the merch in one trusted order.

For organizers:

> Sell the event and everything around it from one branded platform, with inventory, customer orders, payouts and refunds in one place.

The strongest differentiator is not simply that Elora accepts payments. Payment providers already do that. Elora owns the event workflow around the payment: listings, ticket inventory, merch, orders, QR tickets, refunds and reporting.

## What to sell first

### Phase 1: organizer ticketing

The first paid offer should be simple enough to sell and reliable enough to deliver:

- Event page
- Ticket types and inventory limits
- Checkout
- Digital ticket and QR code
- Customer order record
- Basic sales dashboard
- Refund policy settings
- Organizer payout and Elora commission record

### Phase 2: event commerce

After ticketing works reliably:

- Merch catalog and variants
- Cart containing tickets and merch
- Inventory deduction
- Customer order history
- Vendor fulfillment status
- Shipping or event pickup options

### Phase 3: growth and operations

- Promoter links and referral tracking
- Sponsor placements and campaign reporting
- Vendor mini-stores
- Advanced sales analytics
- Event cancellation workflows
- Automated refund ledger
- Multiple payment providers behind one payment interface

## Revenue model

Start with one clear revenue stream and add the others only when the underlying workflow is real.

### 1. Transaction commission: primary

Elora keeps an agreed percentage or fixed fee from each successful ticket or merch order. The organizer or vendor sees the agreed split before publishing the event.

Illustrative model, not a final price:

- Ticket or merch sale: N100,000
- Elora platform commission: 5% = N5,000
- Organizer/vendor share: N95,000
- Payment processing fees: explicitly assigned in the commercial agreement

Do not hide payment fees inside the commission. Show the full ledger so refunds and payouts remain understandable.

### 2. Organizer plans: later

Offer a monthly plan only after organizers use the product repeatedly. Possible tiers:

- **Starter:** event page, ticket sales and basic reporting
- **Growth:** lower transaction rate, merch, promo links and advanced analytics
- **Partner:** custom branding, priority support, sponsor inventory and managed operations

### 3. Paid distribution: later

Featured event placement, campaign support and promoter attribution can become paid services. This should never make the discovery feed feel pay-to-win without clear labeling.

### 4. Vendor and sponsor services: later

Elora can earn from vendor subscriptions, sponsor activation packages or campaign management. These are expansion revenue, not the first pitch.

## Who to pitch first

Begin with independent and growing organizers who run recurring events and feel operational pain:

- Concert and nightlife organizers
- Cultural and creative event producers
- Pop-up markets and lifestyle experiences
- Communities that run repeated paid events
- Small festivals and brand activations

Avoid pitching every seller at once. A focused organizer profile makes the first product, onboarding and social content much clearer.

## What not to promise yet

Until the backend exists, do not market these as completed capabilities:

- Automatic organizer payouts
- Guaranteed refunds
- Inventory synchronization
- Shipping tracking
- Fraud protection
- QR scanning at scale
- Sponsor performance reporting

Use language such as **built for**, **coming next**, or **early access** until each workflow is implemented and tested.

## Content positioning

Every post should fit one of four pillars:

### 1. Discovery

Purpose: attract attendees.

Examples:

- This weekend's events
- New artists, makers and venues
- Event previews
- City guides
- Behind-the-scenes culture content

### 2. Organizer education

Purpose: attract and convert organizers.

Examples:

- Why ticket spreadsheets break at the door
- How to set ticket tiers
- How to write a refund policy
- What organizers should track after a sale
- How ticket and merch bundles increase order value

### 3. Product proof

Purpose: show the platform is useful.

Examples:

- Build an event page with us
- A ticket plus a shirt in one checkout
- A refund ledger explained simply
- QR ticket journey
- Organizer sales dashboard walkthrough

### 4. Culture and trust

Purpose: make Elora memorable and credible.

Examples:

- Organizer interviews
- Vendor stories
- Venue spotlights
- Customer event recaps
- Transparent explanations of fees, payouts and refunds

## Brand voice

Elora should sound:

- Curated, not exclusive
- Confident, not corporate
- Operationally clear, not technical for its own sake
- Excited about culture, but serious about money and trust

Avoid saying only: **We sell tickets.**

Prefer: **Elora gives events the commerce layer they need to sell, fulfill and grow.**

## The first measurable business targets

Do not optimize for followers first. Track:

- Number of qualified organizers contacted
- Number of organizers publishing a real event
- Ticket conversion rate
- Average order value
- Repeat organizer rate
- Refund resolution time
- Merch attach rate to ticket orders
- Net revenue after payment costs and refunds

## Recommended first pitch

> Elora is an event commerce platform for organizers who want more than a payment link. We help you publish your event, sell tickets, add merch, manage customer orders and keep a clear record of commissions, payouts and refunds. For attendees, it is one trusted place to discover the event and buy the complete experience.
